## 3. The three PARTIAL rows — deep-dives (Phase 3)

### 3A. V62-DET-08 — snapshot determinacy: descriptive metadata, not a missing enforcement obligation

**The published text** (§3.10, "Snapshot determinacy") does three things:
it adds the optional companion field `recurringAmountState`; it states
the legacy reading (absent ⇒ the figure reads UNKNOWN); and it imposes
exactly one obligation on consumers — a **prohibition**: "A snapshot
figure MUST NOT be used as enforcement input beyond its stated
determinacy." No clause requires any enforcement path to *consume* the
field, and §3.10's enforcement machinery (determinacy assessment,
licensing, envelope gating) is defined over `CommercialEffect`
determinacy records, a different type from `SubscriptionSnapshot`.

**The code, re-verified.** All five snapshot producers set the field at
construction from what they actually established (Stripe/Paddle/Zuora
sum their modelled items and label DETERMINED; both Chargebee paths sum
only provider-stated/priced amounts, withhold the figure, and label
UNKNOWN when an item price is unestablished — the historical
counterexample in G-07, a DETERMINED label beside a fallback-estimated
figure, is closed at the producer side because the estimating fallbacks
were removed in the migrations). No code path reads
`recurringAmountState` (grep-verified across `src/`), and no enforcement
path consumes a snapshot's `recurringAmount` at all: the snapshot billing
fields that *are* consumed are `currency` and `cadence`, for §6.19 unit
comparability. The §3.10 prohibition is therefore satisfied in the
strongest available way — the figure is never used as enforcement input,
at any determinacy.

**Effect determinacy is established independently of the snapshot.**
Every effect's determinacy is derived at effect construction from that
effect's own evidence and judged by the engine over the effect record
(`determinacyOf`, `assessDimensionSide`; V62-DET-01…07, all directly
pinned), and each adapter derives its committed determinacy from its own
post-commit evidence — never from a snapshot. At no point does a
DETERMINED effect rest on an unverified snapshot figure; the audit
specifically confirms the negative direction of the standing
instruction: nothing derives DETERMINED financial effects from the
snapshot field.

**Verdict: descriptive reader-facing metadata; PARTIAL retained.** The
emission half of the rule is implemented; the row stays PARTIAL because
the field has no enforcement consumer, and the traceability says exactly
that. This is not an implementation defect: inventing a consumer would
create behaviour the published text does not require.

**Governance disposition (recommendation, not enacted).** Two honest
closures exist, both maintainer acts: (i) amend §3.10 to mark the field
explicitly *informative* — a normative-text change on a freeze-guarded
path requiring `protocol-change-approved`, after which the row can be
reclassified against the amended rule; or (ii) direct a named
enforcement consumer in a future revision, with failing-first tests.
Proposed amendment text is recorded in §6 of this audit as an
**unapproved proposal only**. Until one is taken, PARTIAL is the
accurate status and is carried as an accepted experimental limitation
(§4, class B).

### 3B. V62-EVD-01 — evidence authenticity: five layers, separately judged

The merged PR #50 recovery-verifier work and the PR #52/#53 adapter
corrections changed *some* layers below. They do not change the layer
the PARTIAL turns on.

| Layer | Demonstrated? | Evidence and its exact reach |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Internal record consistency | **Yes** | A record cannot contradict itself and pass: test54.1–54.4 (a `defined_equal_exact` label over figures differing from the quote reconciles VIOLATED on zero/generous/no/malformed-envelope paths), test54.7–54.9 (store minting), receipt identity digests (test14–16), checksum-verified restores (test50, test58/59). Proves: labels are judged against the record's own figures. Does not prove: anything about the world outside the record. |
| 2. Operation identity binding | **Yes — inside simulator models only** | Stripe-sim: transition journal + per-operation identity stamped on the dispatched call's response artifact (test61.18/61.19 — same-kind foreign landings denied). Chargebee-sim: per-operation artifacts with begin-binding (test60, #46). Store: evidence tokens bound to quote + subscription + attempt (test56.7, test54.15–54.19). Every one of these identities is minted by the same simulator/store code that consumes it: they demonstrate the *discipline* of operation binding, and are **not** bindings to any real provider operation. |
| 3. Provider-origin authenticity | **No** | Nothing in the core or store authenticates that a provider event occurred. The published text states this itself (§3.11: "A provenance label is not authentication… an adapter asserting it with determinacy established is taken at its classification"), and the maintainer ratified the limitation (G-01, PR #21). A fabricated observation carrying establishing labels and intent-matching state still mints; PR #39/test54 closed the half where the fabrication contradicted the record's own figures, not the half where it is internally consistent. |
| 4. Financial-actual establishment | **Yes, relative to the trusted adapter boundary** | Committed figures establish only from `provider_actual` / `defined_equal_exact` classifications, judged with determinacy (test35), unit comparability (test49), and the §6.15 promotion machinery (test50). The judging is real; its inputs are adapter-supplied records, so establishment inherits layer 3's limit: figures are *judged*, their origin is not *authenticated*. |
| 5. Independent evidence verification | **Partially — independence from the caller, not from the adapter** | The store refuses caller-constructed, unbranded, altered, or cross-bound evidence (test14–16, test50.5–50.12, test31.25). PR #50 hardened the durable-recovery path the same way one level up: no public minting path, records *fetched* by a configured verifier from a deployment-supplied source (never caller-supplied), instance-bound tokens, provider-finality gates, and a fail-closed default — a store with no verifier configured can lift nothing, and the library ships no record source (test58, test59). What this proves: the *caller* cannot fabricate lifting evidence. What it cannot prove: that a deployment's configured source genuinely reads an authenticated provider system — that is an explicit deployment trust decision (§6 of `research/indeterminate-recovery-evidence.md`), and operator identity itself is asserted, not authenticated. The persisted hashes are internal-consistency checksums, not anti-tamper authentication (same disclosed posture as G-05's manifest). |

**Verdict: PARTIAL retained.** Authentication still depends on the
trusted adapter boundary (layer 3), and layers 2 and 5 are demonstrated
only within simulator and deployment-trust boundaries. Simulator-provided
operation identities and branded verifier tokens are **not** proof of
real-provider authentication, and no document in this PR describes them

audit examined every establishment path against that contract:

in each case the hook's contract cannot express a check the adapter

published text names the shared contract as the verification
mechanism, and three paths establish equivalent verdicts outside it;
the accurate claim is equivalence-by-pins, not consumption.
